# Automated usability testing

From [Chapter 13, Findability and Navigation]({{ '/chapters/findability-and-navigation/' | relative_url }}). The chapter mentions this in the tree test and first click sections.
{: .fs-5 }

The best case for automated usability testing is when you want to compare how a large number of participants perform the same well-scripted tasks without any human nuance involved.

There are many automated usability platforms, both explicitly for UX research and as part of other marketing or customer experience research platforms. Any unmoderated testing tool you choose will have detailed instructions for setting up the tests. Our one recommendation: KISS. *Keep It Simple, Stupid*. Complex or multi-part tasks risk confusing participants and producing muddled data.

Think of it like a survey (see [Chapter 10, Surveys]({{ '/chapters/surveys/' | relative_url }})), but with keystroke and mouse logging. It will never achieve the context and nuance of human moderated testing in most situations. So, if you still think automated usability testing will help, here's how to get the most out of it.

## Keep it simple

**Assess one quality at a time.** Without a moderator to ask follow-up questions, each task is more like a survey question than an interview prompt. Clearly define what information each task and question is trying to elicit. Are you assessing capability? Comprehension? You can't mix both.

**Write specific scenarios.** Use a couple of clearly written (and edited, and pre-tested) sentences to give everyone the same clear goal. "Dungeness Crab season is here, and your restaurant needs appropriate cutlery." As always, don't go crazy with the details. It's a task, not an adventure. Include all the information needed for the task, such as quantities or specific attributes. Unless it's an important part of the task for them to calculate numbers themselves, just give them the values. "Find 75 crab crackers and 150 seafood picks and prepare the order for checkout."

{: .note }
AI tools are increasingly good at writing tasks. We encourage you to explore them. Some AI models can generate task ideas from a mockup or a screenshot; some from a text description or the research plan. For every dozen AI-generated tasks, we find that perhaps two are usable with some additional editing. But that's two tasks we didn't have before! Sometimes, we just use them for a little extra inspiration.
