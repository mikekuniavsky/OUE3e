# Accessibility resources

For [Chapter 8, Usability Testing]({{ '/chapters/usability-testing/' | relative_url }}). The chapter doesn't cover standards-based accessibility evaluation. It points here instead.
{: .fs-5 }

Accessibility testing has its own tools and its own specialists: automated code checks, manual inspections, walkthroughs with screen readers and other assistive technology, and assessments of how complex your content is. If you want to get started, these are free.

## Standards

- [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/), from the W3C Web Accessibility Initiative. The chapter's Box 8.5 summarizes its four principles: perceivable, operable, understandable and robust.
- [Introduction to web accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-intro/), also from the W3C.

## United States

- [Section508.gov](https://www.section508.gov/) has guidance for building and testing accessible digital products, including [how to test](https://www.section508.gov/test/).

## United Kingdom

- GOV.UK Service Manual: [Making your service accessible: an introduction](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/making-your-service-accessible-an-introduction).
- GOV.UK Service Manual: [Testing for accessibility](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/testing-for-accessibility).

## Screen readers

For a quick check on your own, the chapter suggests using a screen reader. The free [NVDA](https://www.nvaccess.org/) (Windows) and VoiceOver (built into iOS and macOS) will cover the majority of users.
