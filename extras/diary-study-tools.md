# Tools for online diary studies

For [Chapter 11, Diary Studies]({{ '/chapters/diary-studies/' | relative_url }}). The chapter describes the options. This page gathers them in one place, with links.
{: .fs-5 }

Tools change faster than books. The list below is what we wrote in 2024. Check each one before you commit to it.

| Option | What it's good for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Email** | The simplest digital tool. Email participants a form and have them reply with their answers. They can attach photos and video. Free, and familiar. | Any email account |
| **Email to blog** | Replies post automatically to a blog or group that you (and your client) can follow, and you can use comments to talk with the participant about an entry. | [WhatsApp](https://www.whatsapp.com/), [WordPress](https://wordpress.com/) |
| **Spreadsheet** | Structured data. You name the columns, and respondents fill in the rows. | Any online or desktop spreadsheet |
| **Form-filling tools** | Diary forms that people complete by "responding" to a survey. Send a link to a new "survey" by text or email each day, and use the built-in analytics. Include a free text field where participants identify themselves. | [SurveyMonkey](https://www.surveymonkey.com/), [Google Forms](https://forms.google.com/), [Typeform](https://www.typeform.com/) |
| **Custom diary platforms** | A diary app for participants to download, a library of standard exercises, photo and video capture, and built-in tools to aggregate and analyze. Usually not free, and less flexible than assembling your own. | [dscout](https://dscout.com/), [Forsta](https://www.forsta.com/) |
| **Participants' existing apps** | Not a tool so much as a strategy. Participants give you temporary access to the social tools they already use, and you watch what they would normally share with friends. The researcher Chris Khalil calls this *lifestreaming*. | Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Pinterest, TikTok, Twitter, Twitch, Discord, text messaging |

## Two cautions from the chapter

{: .warning }
If you use an email-to-web solution, password-protect access to the resulting website. Do the same for every other online tool. Don't let participants see each other's data, and don't make data publicly viewable. Your participants didn't sign up to have the details of their lives exposed.

{: .note }
If you collect video or voice entries, a mailbox service with AI transcription can save you time.
