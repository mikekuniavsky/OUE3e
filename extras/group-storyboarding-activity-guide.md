# A sample activity guide: group storyboarding

From [Chapter 12, Working with Groups]({{ '/chapters/working-with-groups/' | relative_url }}). It goes with the "anatomy of a group activity guide" section.
{: .fs-5 }

This is the third exercise in a series. In the first, the group defined personas. In the second, they generated hypotheses about multi-touchpoint services. The exercise is 25 minutes long, and there is a lot of underlying structure to make a chaotic drawing and gluing sprint produce relevant results.

| | What you say | Why you say it |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead-in** | It's time to tell a story about how the persona you created in the first activity will interact with the product idea you had in the second. We will collaboratively create storyboards. | Explain the purpose of the activity, and emphasize that you are interested in the participants' personal experiences. |
| **Key constraints** | Storyboards are short cartoon stories that describe a persona's interaction with a product or service. Like any story, a scenario has a beginning, middle and end. The beginning should have the persona identify and describe the challenge facing her or him, from his or her perspective. The middle should describe how he or she struggles with the challenge, using the application to address some aspect of it. The end should show how the situation is resolved. There are very few magic solutions in the world, so the odds are that most of the problem will still be there, but that the application made some aspect of it easier. | Describe what is and isn't part of the goals of the activity. |
| **Specific instructions** | To create a storyboard we want you to draw a sequence of states where a persona uses a solution idea to address a specific need, goal or problem. Treat this like a comic strip. Feel free to use stick figures, cutouts, etc. Write descriptions below each panel. <br>• As a group, talk through a sample scenario for the persona (5 minutes)<br>• Split up and draw 6 panels in small groups (18 minutes)<br>• Put all the panels together into a single narrative (2 minutes) | Keep instructions simple and short. |
| **Set rules** | Every group today has people in it who don't know each other well. We trust you to organize yourselves, divide responsibility and give everyone an opportunity to contribute. However, in the interest of speed, you do not need to agree on every question. If a discussion begins to get complicated, we ask you to take a vote to choose a direction. If your idea is not selected, don't worry, it's only an exercise and it'll be over soon. | Describe the expectations for participants: how they should expect to behave with each other and how they should manage the social dynamics. |
| **Hints** | <br>• STICK FIGURES! Do not worry if you can't draw. You can draw a stick figure and make a word balloon, and that's enough.<br>• We recommend drawing individual panels on big stickies in small groups and arranging them at the end.<br>• Focus on actions, but feel free to include dialog, thought bubbles and transitions between one state and the next to describe the experience. | Give people hints about where to go. These are not instructions, so we don't frame them as such, but they gently guide people away from common anxieties when doing an unfamiliar activity. |
| **Moderator instructions** | <br>• Do not let people from the same working group sit together.<br>• In between sessions: verify every table has the following supplies: 2 packs of multi-color sharpies, medium sized sticky notes, poster-sized sticky notes, sticker sheets.<br>• Make sure everyone is contributing. Give people sharpies and paper who are not drawing. | Moderator instructions are there to remind the moderator to keep the activity focused. |

**At the end, wrap up.** "Wow, that was a great storyboarding sprint! Congratulations everyone! Now we really have to wrap up and move on to the next activity." At the end of the session, after thanking everyone for coming, give participants a way to contact you if they have any more questions or thoughts. And you're done!
