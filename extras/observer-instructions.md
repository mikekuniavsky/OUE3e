# Instructions for observers

For [Chapter 8, Usability Testing]({{ '/chapters/usability-testing/' | relative_url }}) and [Chapter 12, Working with Groups]({{ '/chapters/working-with-groups/' | relative_url }}).
{: .fs-5 }

Send this to everyone who will watch a session, ahead of time, and go over it with them before the first one starts. Copy it, cut it down and change it to fit. It's drawn from what we say in the chapters.

## Why you're here

Watching real people use something is the fastest way to understand what they run into. It's harder than it looks. You're here to see what happens and write it down, not to fix anything yet.

## What to expect

You'll watch several sessions. Each person will do roughly the same tasks, and you'll see them stumble in places that look obvious to you. That's the point. The moderator will debrief with you right after each session, so save your reactions for then.

## While the session is running

- **Stay quiet.** It's hard not to comment on what's happening. If you chat during a session, you'll miss the small things the participant does. Write your comments down and share them in the debrief.
- **Don't interrupt.** You may want to ask the participant a question, and some of your questions will be good ones. Interrupting the moderator throws off the session, though. Send your question to the moderator privately (a back channel in your chat tool works well) and they'll decide when to ask it.
- **Don't introduce yourself, explain or help.** The more honest the participant is, the better. If they know you built the thing, they tend to soften their criticism. Please don't offer help, correct a misunderstanding or explain the value of the product. When someone gets stuck, that's the finding.
- **Watch what people do, not just what they say.** Note where they hesitate, sigh, squint or click the wrong thing.
- **Mark the good moments.** Note the time when something interesting happens so we can find it again for a highlight reel.

## Between sessions

- **Hold off on designing solutions** until you've seen all the participants. It's natural to want to fix a problem as soon as you see it. We need to know how big it is and how often it happens first. Try not to let the first participant set the tone.
- **Try not to jump to conclusions.** One person's trouble is a lead, not a result.

## After each session

We'll debrief right away. Bring your notes. We want to hear what you saw that the moderator may have missed.
